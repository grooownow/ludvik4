A website or web app development quote describes the work needed to turn a
business problem into something people can use: understand an offer, send an
enquiry, book an appointment or complete a useful task. Its price reflects the
decisions, implementation and checks required to deliver that agreed result.

The finished screen can look deceptively simple. A service page, a booking form,
a small customer portal. After trying an AI coding tool, it is reasonable to
ask why development costs what it does when a similar-looking screen can appear
from a prompt.

Consider a hypothetical consultation business. A visitor selects a time, enters
their details and receives confirmation. For the owner, a working booking system
also means unavailable slots cannot be selected, a second click does not create
a duplicate appointment, notifications arrive and the schedule can be changed.
Those requirements determine the work behind the screen.

## The decisions behind the price

### Making the offer and customer journey clear

Before choosing a layout, someone needs to establish what visitors should
understand and what they should do next. A consultant selling personal sessions
and a software business offering self-service signup need different information,
even when both websites fit on a single page.

That work may include reviewing the audience, buying questions, alternatives and
existing material. A visitor asking why a service costs what it does needs its
scope and evidence explained. Someone asking whether it suits them needs clear
conditions and examples.

Research should produce usable decisions: a page structure, first-release
priorities and a direction for the copy. Ask what the proposed research includes.
A review of existing material, customer interviews and a separate market study
are different commitments. The size of the presentation alone says little about
their value.

### A coherent design system

Design includes typography, spacing, colour, imagery and the behaviour of
interface elements. These choices should express the business and help people
read, choose and act.

Consistency also helps future changes. When buttons, cards and spacing follow
shared rules, a new service page has an established visual language to use.
It can fit naturally into the existing website.

Mobile design requires decisions about how content reorganises and what stays
prominent. Accessibility adds concrete checks:
[W3C's WCAG guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/quickref/) covers contrast,
keyboard operation and error identification. Agree which interface checks are
included in the project.

“Custom design” becomes a useful line in a quote when it specifies the directions
to be explored, the pages and states to be designed, and how feedback is handled.

### A complete working journey

Development connects the interface to actions and data. For a website, that
might mean delivering an enquiry. For an application, it can include saving a
booking, performing a calculation and showing the result to the right user.

Our consultation business could link to an existing booking service or commission
its own scheduling system. The visitor's first screen might look similar. The
custom system, however, needs rules for availability, stored appointments,
permissions, notifications and failures. That extra work needs a business reason.

Describe functionality through actions: choose a time, submit details, receive
confirmation, change a booking. A label such as “customer portal” leaves too much
of the intended behaviour unexplained.

### Checks that support future changes

A usable product needs its normal journey checked alongside situations that
interrupt it: invalid details, an unavailable external service, a repeated action
or an empty list. Important behaviour benefits from repeatable checks that can
reveal a problem after the next update.

This is where code quality becomes relevant to the owner. A pricing rule has a
clear home. Changes can be traced. There is a way to verify that appointment
booking still works after a new feature. Another developer can understand the
project and continue it.

The depth should match the task. A straightforward informational website can
have a simple structure and focused checks. An application with users and stored
appointments needs more attention to data and failure handling.

### Launch and handover

The quote should explain who publishes the result, connects the domain, checks
the live environment and transfers access. For an application holding data,
backup and recovery responsibilities also need to be defined.

Handover should cover the source code, agreed accounts and operating instructions:
how to update the project, edit content, identify its external services and get
help with a problem. Record ownership and licensing arrangements explicitly.

Running costs continue after launch: the domain, hosting, external services,
maintenance and future changes. Keep those visible alongside the initial project
price so you can assess the commitment over time.

## How the estimate becomes a price

An estimate begins with a defined scope. Each part is assessed for complexity,
skills, expected effort and dependencies. The price then accounts for the work,
specialist contributions where needed and agreed external costs.

A fixed-price proposal also accounts for responsibility for the agreed result
and identified risks. If an integration's behaviour is unknown, a focused
feasibility check can establish what is possible before the full implementation
is priced. You should be able to see which costs are established and which depend
on that investigation.

There is no universal percentage for design or reliable price per page. A page
with supplied content and a familiar contact flow involves different work from
one with original artwork, a calculator and several languages. Two small
applications can likewise differ substantially in their permission and data rules.

A higher price does not establish quality on its own. The amount needs an
explanation in terms of scope, delivery boundaries and verification.

## Compare proposals by what they deliver

Imagine two proposals for the consultation business. One offers a template-based
page linked to an existing calendar. The other offers an original website and a
custom booking system with an administration area.

The first could fully solve the problem and be the better starting point. The
second makes sense when the existing calendar cannot meet a specific business
requirement, such as unusual availability rules or a connected internal process.
Every additional custom feature also adds something to maintain.

Before comparing totals, ask each provider to explain:

- which content and decisions you need to supply;
- which pages, actions and states are included;
- where existing services are used and what their ongoing costs are;
- how the work is checked and accepted;
- which revisions are included and how scope changes are priced;
- which accounts and source files you receive;
- who handles defects after launch, and on what terms.

Technical SEO also deserves a clear definition. Titles, metadata and crawlability
help search engines understand a website.
[Google's starter guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide)
states that inclusion in its index is not guaranteed. Ongoing promotion and
customer acquisition need their own scope.

## How AI changes the economics

Codex, Claude Code and other tools can be used during implementation. When
individual tasks become faster, that can create room for more exploration,
reduce the budget or bring forward delivery of an agreed scope. The effect
depends on the project; a universal discount would be difficult to justify.

Product decisions remain: what to build, which rules matter, how to verify it and
who will maintain it. With a fixed price, compare the complete outcome and
handover terms. With hourly work, clarify how time spent using AI is recorded.

## Where a smaller budget can still work

Start by simplifying the first release. Use an existing booking service when it
fits. Deliver one complete journey. Prepare accurate source material. Agree the
structure before implementation. Defer a feature that has no clear user yet.
