# International Market Strategy

## Strategy in one paragraph

Ludvik4 should compete as a small, senior, founder-led studio for bounded web
outcomes, not as a low-cost generalist developer and not as a miniature agency.
The commercial entry points are three high-intent service pages; the home page
explains the shared delivery model; public work and open-source artefacts lower
trust risk. Distribution starts where a small studio can show inspectable work:
GitHub/Codex ecosystems, founder communities, selected marketplace profiles,
and practical problem-led content. Search supports discovery over time but is
not treated as the only acquisition channel.

## Market evidence

Clutch's August 2025 US small-business survey reports that 83% of small
businesses had a website, 90% planned website investment during the following
12 months, and the leading priorities included redesign, performance, mobile
experience, content, SEO, security, and payments. It also shows a mature DIY
alternative: 41% used no-code and 34% low-code, while 12% used custom
development. The implication is not “every business needs custom code”; the
website offer must win on offer clarity, integration, ownership, and accountable
delivery where builders are insufficient.

## Positioning

Canonical one-liner:

> Founder-led website, workflow automation, and web app development — from an
> unclear problem to a focused, production-ready release.

Message hierarchy:

1. What is built: websites, workflow automations, MVPs, and web apps.
2. For whom: founders and small teams with a real business problem.
3. How risk is reduced: explicit scope, one complete journey, tests and
   controls, direct accountability.
4. Why believe it: public work, source code, documentation, and a concrete
   delivery process.
5. Why AI matters: it improves the delivery toolchain without removing
   verification.

Avoid leading with `digital products`. It is a useful umbrella but a weak
retrieval and buying phrase on its own. Avoid leading with AI: generic AI agency
copy is crowded and can make websites and conventional web apps look secondary.

## Offer architecture

| Offer | Buyer trigger | Decision unit | Conversion promise |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Website design and development | unclear/dated offer, launch, campaign | one audience + offer + primary action | a responsive site ready to publish and measure |
| Workflow automation | repeated manual work and fragile handoffs | one process with a trigger and outcome | a controlled workflow, not an isolated script |
| MVP / custom web app | product idea or missing operational tool | one problem + complete user journey | a production-ready first release with source and deployment |

Prices remain proposal-based. After a short enquiry review, the user receives a
recommended next step: direct fixed-scope proposal or paid discovery when the
process/product is not yet defined. Do not add “starting from” amounts without
separate approval and evidence from international sales.

## Acquisition sequence

### P1 — owned conversion and proof

- Maintain the home, three service pages, About, Work, qa-pilot case, privacy,
  sitemap, and machine-readable source files.
- Make GitHub profiles and qa-pilot attribution use the canonical one-liner and
  point to the most relevant EN page.
- Package the three services consistently on Contra; use Upwork Project Catalog
  only where scope and revision boundaries can be stated honestly.

### P2 — narrow, evidence-led distribution

- Publish technical/product walkthroughs derived from real work: how scope is
  reduced, how automation failures are controlled, and how tests prove a first
  release.
- Participate in founder and developer communities with the artefact first and
  service link second.
- Build one reusable capability deck/proposal from the product canon; do not
  create channel-specific positioning drift.

### P3 — search content and external consensus

- Publish the first six support pages in `search-and-content-strategy.md` only
  when each can contain first-hand examples, diagrams, checklists, or code.
- Seek a small number of relevant third-party profiles/directories with the same
  brand definition. No bulk directory submissions or artificial mentions.
- Add comparison pages only after real enquiries show a repeated alternative or
  objection.

## Measurement

Primary business metric: qualified project enquiries by landing page and
service type.

Supporting metrics:

- indexed P1 pages / submitted P1 pages;
- non-brand impressions and qualified clicks per service page;
- enquiry start and successful submission by landing page;
- GitHub → site and site → GitHub assisted paths;
- proposal rate and win/loss reason by service;
- LLM discovery, understanding, selection, and citation as separate monthly
  observations, never as one “visibility score”.

The EN privacy notice currently states that non-essential analytics are off.
The code therefore hard-disables PostHog in the EN build. Enquiry attribution
must remain server/log or privacy-compatible until consent and the notice are
deliberately changed.
